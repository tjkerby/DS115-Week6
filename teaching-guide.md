# Week 6 teaching guide

## Purpose

Orient students to work and further education after their undergraduate degree, connect preparation with responsibilities, and leave time for their questions. Explain general possibilities first, then add your own experiences to reinforce or complicate them.

## Timing for 50 minutes

| Minutes | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | One-slide orientation beyond the undergraduate degree |
| 2-18 | Graduate preparation, research independence, NSF comparison, and leadership example |
| 18-28 | Research environments and considerations for graduate school |
| 28-30 | Partner discussion about information needed to evaluate an option |
| 30-33 | Initial notes for the final reflection |
| 33-38 | Anonymous course survey and question submission |
| 38-48 | Student questions |
| 48-50 | Closing and existing assignment reminders |

There are 15 main slides and three optional reference slides. The first eight slides of the original draft are now one opening slide. Use the saved time for a fuller degree discussion and student questions. If needed, shorten the partner exchange and personal examples to preserve the last 20 minutes.

## Important distinctions

- A first job is a starting point. Degree level does not fix a career ceiling.
- Research independence, technical leadership, and organizational leadership are related but different responsibilities.
- Bachelor's and master's holders in the NSF computer/math occupation group report similar rates of R&D as their primary activity. Doctorate holders report a higher rate. The data do not establish causation or measure leadership.
- The Google role illustrates an advertised experience-based route to significant technical responsibility. It does not establish typical hiring outcomes.
- Industry's share of notable AI model development does not measure its share of all research.
- Evaluate the actual program, employer, colleagues, and working conditions rather than relying on degree or job titles alone.

## Personal examples

Optional places for your own examples are noted on the slides about doctoral training and research environments. Choose brief examples that follow the general explanation. No personal anecdotes have been invented.

## Reflection and survey

Give students three minutes to begin notes for the supplied five-question final reflection. Their final submission stays in Canvas. Then give five minutes for the separate anonymous survey. Its third question deliberately connects to the reflection's unresolved question. Students may refine their reflection after the class discussion.

## Discussion

Read the question submissions privately, group similar ones, and paraphrase. Invite spoken follow-ups. If none arrive, use the optional backup questions after the closing slide. Distinguish personal experience, evidence, and program-specific information that needs checking.
